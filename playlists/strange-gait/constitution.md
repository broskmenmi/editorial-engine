# STRANGE GAIT — Constitution

> **Working title only. The public name is under reconsideration.**

This volume is interested in **techno that does not behave as if one correct form exists**.

Its selection lens is rhythmic and structural freedom: groove that can bend, slip, swing, fracture, straighten, dissolve, become sparse, become dense, or otherwise resist a single expected grammar.

The point is not to prove that every track is weird. The point is to keep the door open to music that feels physically convincing without obeying a narrow idea of what "proper techno" should be.

Repository-level `LONG-FORM-DJ-DOCTRINE.md` governs the actual journey and sequencing. That global doctrine overrides any older wording in this volume that treated flow as sameness or required one sustained sonic room.

## Selection lens

Prefer tracks that offer one or more of these without turning them into a checklist:

- strong bodily pull;
- unusual or ambiguous groove;
- swing, shuffle, displacement or syncopation;
- broken or tilted four-to-the-floor;
- polyrhythm, polymeter or competing pulse suggestions;
- elastic low-end relationships;
- organic percussion interacting with machine precision;
- negative space that changes the perceived pulse;
- loops that mutate without announcing the trick;
- repetition that becomes physical rather than merely functional;
- unexpected simplicity after complexity, or vice versa;
- structural choices that resist formula while remaining musically convincing.

A straight track can belong. A melodic track can belong. A dubby, warm, hard, sparse, funky, raw, hypnotic, tribal, broken or minimal track can belong if it contributes to the volume's broader curiosity and works somewhere in the journey.

## What this volume is not

This is not:

- a catalogue of different groove techniques;
- a quota for asymmetry, polymeter or rhythmic cleverness;
- an IDM showcase;
- a purity test for "real techno";
- a requirement that every track occupy the same vibe or pressure envelope;
- permission for random sequencing merely because disruption sounds interesting on paper.

The volume should remain inviting rather than elitist. Technical language is useful for describing what music does, not for signalling who understands techno correctly.

## Flow and journey

The canonical sequence follows `LONG-FORM-DJ-DOCTRINE.md`.

Different vibes are allowed.

Energy changes are allowed.

A left turn is allowed.

A track may deliberately break the established grammar.

What matters is whether the move feels earned and whether the larger journey retains taste.

Do not reject a placement merely because the next track feels different. Reject or revisit it when the difference feels arbitrary, mistimed, or damaging to the surrounding run and long-form arc.

## No role progression quota

Do not map successive tracks mechanically to `Ground → Tilt → Lock → Fracture → Deep Lock → Escape`.

Those labels may still be used when they genuinely help explain part of a journey, but they are optional editorial vocabulary rather than a required story model.

Likewise, the first three tracks do not need to remain one vibe. They need to establish that the sequence is being curated deliberately.

## Tempo

Default discovery territory may still often sit around **128–146 BPM**, but this is a search convenience, not the volume's identity.

BPM never proves energy or flow.

Large tempo changes require more scrutiny, but are not globally prohibited when a convincing metrical relation, intentional reset, transition design, or listener evidence supports them.

Unknown perceived pulse remains unknown.

## Evidence authority

Keep separate:

1. **Listener report** — direct volunteered experience of the actual sequence.
2. **Measured evidence** — BPM, duration, identity, order, lawful audio measurements.
3. **Attributed description** — artist, label or reviewer statements.
4. **Craft convention** — useful DJ practice, not law.
5. **Editorial interpretation** — the engine's hypothesis about role, flow or meaning.

Spotify metadata is not raw audio. Never infer energy, density, swing, hypnosis, emotional temperature, phrase structure or mixability from metadata alone.

## Listener authority

Listening is leisure, not QA.

- Never assign mandatory A/B tests, rankings, prescribed listening sessions, or homework.
- Natural reactions are enough.
- Listener reports about flow, energy, boredom, excitement, awkwardness, surprise or taste may reopen any paper-perfect sequence.
- A complaint is evidence, not authorization unless the user approves the required action scope.

## Opener and protected state

No opener, handoff, ending, chapter or track is protected at creation time.

Protection must be earned through explicit user instruction or positive listener evidence.

## Final test

For a track, ask:

**Is this music worth having available for this volume's broader techno curiosity?**

For a placement, ask:

**Does this feel like a DJ making a deliberate move, or like another good track was simply queued?**

The first question belongs to this constitution.

The second belongs to the global long-form DJ doctrine.
