# GROOVE OVER NOISE — Editorial Notes

## Structural principles

- Sequence for a long-form listening journey, not a live DJ set.
- Require bodily pulse and attention continuity; beatmatching is not required.
- Target adjacent BPM differences of 0–4 BPM.
- Flag 5–7 BPM differences for stronger evidence.
- Reject differences above 7 BPM unless half-time/double-time equivalence or an intentional reset is documented.
- Apply the strictest standard to the opening three tracks.
- BPM compliance screens transitions but never proves they feel right.
- Avoid accidental tempo sawtoothing.
- Prefer continuity over isolated highlights.
- Increase density before intensity.
- Preserve decompression and dissolution at the end.
- Spotify Mix cannot repair or validate a defective sequence.
- Volunteered listener feedback is direct audit evidence.

## Relaxation-first policy

The workflow exists to make music discovery relaxing.

The user is not expected to compare alternatives, rank candidates, confirm chapter roles, or listen on demand. The system makes the best available decision, carries uncertainty internally, continues discovering, and learns from reactions that arise naturally during ordinary listening.

`MANUAL ACTION` is technical only and must never contain listening work.

## Evidence model

Every material claim belongs to one of four categories:

1. **Measured evidence:** BPM, duration, exact identity, sequence position, or lawful audio measurements.
2. **Craft convention:** useful sequencing practice rather than a universal musical law.
3. **Listener report:** the user's volunteered experience.
4. **Editorial interpretation:** a working role such as re-entry, crest, summit, decompression, or dissolution.

Measurements constrain decisions but do not replace listening. Do not infer sonic or emotional qualities from metadata alone.

## Long-form house style

The preferred shape is:

`Arrival → Groove formation → Local crest → Partial release → Re-entry → Deeper crest → Partial release → Main summit → Long decompression → Dissolution`

This is the GROOVE OVER NOISE house style, not a universal techno rule.

- Several local crests are allowed; one summit should dominate.
- Later crests must reveal a new quality rather than repeat the same build louder.
- Partial releases preserve accumulated immersion.
- Re-entry must not sound like a restart.
- The summit is not defined by BPM alone.
- New chapters normally belong before the protected final descent.
- Natural listener feedback overrides the planned story.

## Candidate verdicts

- **ADD** — best available evidence supports admission now; narrative labels may remain provisional.
- **REVISIT** — a concrete material uncertainty remains without creating user homework.
- **REJECT** — weakens, duplicates, or creates a transition, attention, or storytelling defect.
- **UNDER REVIEW** — an accepted track has an actual listener-reported or objective problem.
